To approach this biomedical regression problem, I have outlined a specific plan based on the techniques and functions detailed in the course materials.
Phase 1: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
The goal is to identify batch effects, multicollinearity, and data distribution issues that inform model choice.
Data Summaries: I will use df.describe() to check the range and scale of the 40 gene features. For skewed distributions (common in gene expression), I will use sns.histplot() to identify right-skewness; if skewness is high (>6), a log-transformation will be applied to help linear models capture the signal.
Structure and Batch Effects: Because laboratories may share "batch effects," I will use Principal Components Analysis (PCA) (sklearn.decomposition.PCA) to project the 40 features into a 2D space. Visualizing the PCA scatter plot colored by group_id will reveal if samples cluster by laboratory, indicating significant batch effects.
Correlation Check: Using df.corr() and sns.heatmap(), I will identify multicollinearity between genes. High correlation suggests that a dimension-reduction approach like PCR or a shrinkage method like Ridge may be necessary to stabilize coefficient estimates.
Phase 2: Model Development and Interpretation
I will train two models that handle high-dimensional, correlated data differently to compare their performance.
Model 1: Lasso Regression (L1 Penalty):
Reasoning: Lasso is ideal for gene expression because it performs automatic variable selection. By shrinking irrelevant gene coefficients to exactly zero, it provides a sparse, interpretable model.
Implementation: I will use sklearn.linear_model.Lasso within a Pipeline that includes StandardScaler.
Model 2: Principal Components Regression (PCR):
Reasoning: PCR is effective when predictors are highly correlated. It transforms the 40 genes into a smaller set of orthogonal components that explain the most variability.
Implementation: I will use a Pipeline combining PCA(n_components=M) and LinearRegression().
Interpretation: For Lasso, I will inspect the coef_ attribute to identify which specific genes are most predictive. For PCR, I will examine the explained_variance_ratio_ to determine how much biological signal is captured by the top components.
Phase 3: Validation and Model Selection
The validation strategy must mimic the test scenario: predicting on laboratories not seen during training.
Validation Strategy (Grouped Cross-Validation): I will implement a cross-validation strategy that ensures entire laboratories are left out in each fold. Standard K-Fold would leak laboratory-specific "batch effects" into the validation set, leading to an overly optimistic error estimate.
Hyperparameter Tuning: I will use GridSearchCV to select the optimal λ (regularization strength) for Lasso and the optimal M (number of components) for PCR.
Model Selection: The model with the lowest mean cross-validated MSE will be selected. If performance is similar, I will use the "one-standard-error rule" to select the more parsimonious (simpler) model to reduce the risk of overfitting.
Phase 4: Test Prediction
Final Fit: Once the best model and hyperparameters are chosen, I will refit the model using the entire training dataset (all 40 laboratories) to capture the maximum available information.
Prediction Generation: I will use the .predict() method on the test dataset to generate continuous response values. The quality of these predictions will be evaluated using the final test MSE.
